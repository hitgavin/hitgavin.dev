"Flying car" is Musk's framing; an aviation expert told CNN it is closer to a
hop. The mechanism is the interesting part. Tesla's Lars Moravy puts the rear
thrusters at more than 80 kN — the same order as a Dassault Falcon 10X — paired
with a suction system that claims 0–60 mph in 0.81 s at up to 5 g.

If you work on attitude control, rewatch the landing rather than the launch.
The car leaves a ramp inverted, flips mid-air, and arrests its own descent on
thrusters. That is a short-horizon attitude and altitude problem solved on an
actuator with no continuous authority, which is a genuinely harder control
problem than the acceleration number everyone is quoting.

Production is promised for 2028, by a programme that promised deliveries in
2020. Treat the date accordingly.
